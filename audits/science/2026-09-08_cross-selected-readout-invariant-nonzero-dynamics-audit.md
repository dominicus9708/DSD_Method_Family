## 1. Purpose

This audit tests whether a selected readout or invariant remaining constant is sufficient to infer that the underlying state/configuration has not changed.

The target firewall is

\[
\boxed{
\text{selected readout/invariant constancy}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{zero state transition}.
}
\]

A second control tests whether equality of the full endpoint state at two sampled times is sufficient to infer that no dynamics occurred in between.

The audit uses standard quantum mechanics and special relativity as independent external comparators. No quantum-gravity, alternative-gravity, or new DSD physical premise is introduced.

## 3. Exact map-level theorem — fiber motion

Let

\[
\Phi:S\to Y
\]

be a selected readout and let

\[
\Gamma:S\to S
\]

be a transition.

For any \(s\in S\),

\[
\Phi(\Gamma(s))=\Phi(s)
\]

if and only if

\[
\Gamma(s)\in \Phi^{-1}(\Phi(s)).
\]

Therefore selected-output constancy states only that the transition remains inside the same readout fiber.

It does **not** imply

\[
\Gamma(s)=s
\]

unless the relevant fiber is a singleton, or more generally unless \(\Phi\) is injective on the declared orbit/target set.

For a family of readouts \(\{\Phi_i\}_{i\in I}\), define the joint readout

\[
\Phi_*(s)=(\Phi_i(s))_{i\in I}.
\]

If \(\Phi_*\) separates points on the declared orbit, then constancy of all selected outputs over that orbit is sufficient to infer state constancy there.

Thus the exact common statement is

\[
\boxed{
\text{constant selected output}
=\text{same readout fiber},
}
\]

not

\[
\boxed{
\text{constant selected output}
=\text{same full state}.
}
\]

This is an elementary theorem about maps and fibers, not a new physical law.

## 4. Generic finite witness

Take

\[
S=\mathbb R^2,
\qquad
\Phi(x,y)=x^2+y^2,
\]

and let \(\Gamma\) be a rotation by \(\pi/2\).

Then

\[
(1,0)\mapsto(0,1),
\]

while

\[
\Phi(1,0)=\Phi(0,1)=1.
\]

The state changes but the selected readout does not.

A separating readout such as the identity map detects the change immediately.

### Endpoint-return control

A full \(2\pi\) rotation returns

\[
(1,0)\mapsto(1,0),
\]

but passes through distinct intermediate states.

Therefore even

\[
\Gamma_T(s)=s
\]

at one later endpoint does not imply

\[
\Gamma_t(s)=s
\quad\text{for all }0<t<T.
\]

Endpoint equality is weaker than trajectory constancy.

## 5. Standard-QM witness

Use units \(\hbar=1\) and

\[
H=\frac12 Z,
\qquad
|\psi(0)\rangle=|+\rangle
=\frac{|0\rangle+|1\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
\]

The standard unitary evolution is

\[
|\psi(t)\rangle
=e^{-iHt}|+\rangle
=\frac{e^{-it/2}|0\rangle+e^{it/2}|1\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
\]

For all \(t\), the Z-basis probabilities are

\[
P(0)=P(1)=\frac12.
\]

The energy expectation is also constant:

\[
\langle H\rangle=0.
\]

However

\[
\langle X\rangle_t=\cos t.
\]

At

\[
t=\frac\pi2,
\]

we have

\[
\langle X\rangle:1\to0,
\]

and the physical ray differs from the initial ray; the squared overlap is

\[
|\langle\psi(0)|\psi(\pi/2)\rangle|^2=\frac12.
\]

Thus

```text
constant Z-basis probability readout:
  YES

constant energy expectation:
  YES

unchanged quantum state/ray:
  NO
```

This is a standard-QM witness of motion within the fiber of selected readouts.

### Periodic endpoint control

At \(t=2\pi\), the state vector differs only by an overall phase, so the physical ray returns to the initial ray.

At the intermediate time \(t=\pi\), it is a different ray.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\text{same physical quantum endpoint}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{zero intervening unitary dynamics}.
}
\]

No DSD-specific quantum prediction is inferred from this standard example.

## 6. Standard-relativity witness

Use flat spacetime with \(c=1\), speed

\[
v=0.6,
\qquad
\gamma=\frac1{\sqrt{1-v^2}}=1.25.
\]

The inertial four-velocity is

\[
U^\mu=(1.25,0.75,0,0).
\]

Its Minkowski norm is

\[
\eta_{\mu\nu}U^\mu U^\nu
=-1.25^2+0.75^2
=-1.
\]

This selected invariant remains constant along the worldline.

With proper-time parameter \(\tau\), choose the origin at \(\tau=0\). Then

\[
x^\mu(\tau)=U^\mu\tau.
\]

At \(\tau=2\),

\[
x^\mu=(2.5,1.5,0,0),
\]

which is a different spacetime event even though the selected four-velocity norm remains \(-1\).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{constant relativistic invariant}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{unchanged worldline event/state record}.
}
\]

The invariant normalization and the physical meaning of the worldline are supplied by standard relativity, not derived from DSD.

## 7. DSD interpretation

The result strengthens the already established static/dynamic separation.

For an explicitly declared DSD readout/reduction map \(\Phi\),

\[
\Phi(\Sigma_{t'})=\Phi(\Sigma_t)
\]

is not sufficient to infer

\[
\Sigma_{t'}=\Sigma_t
\]

unless the selected readout is reconstructively sufficient for the declared target.

Likewise,

\[
\Sigma_{t'}=\Sigma_t
\]

at two sampled endpoints is not sufficient to infer a constant trajectory between them.

The sharper hierarchy is

\[
\boxed{
\text{selected-output equality}
<
\text{endpoint state equality}
<
\text{trajectory constancy},
}
\]

where the symbols indicate increasing information requirements rather than numerical magnitude.

A safe DSD reporting rule is therefore:

```text
selected static/readout equality
  -> same selected fiber only;

full state equality at sampled endpoints
  -> same endpoints only;

zero dynamics over an interval
  -> requires trajectory/transition information or an independently justified
     separating/injective state description over that interval.
```

This is consistent with SC-05 reconstruction restraint and with the existing separation between static aggregation and Structural Reorganization Dynamics.

## 9. Status classification

### Mathematical / structural theorem layer

- \(\Phi(\Gamma(s))=\Phi(s)\) iff \(\Gamma(s)\) remains in the same \(\Phi\)-fiber.
- Injectivity/separation on the declared orbit is sufficient to turn complete readout constancy into state constancy.
- Endpoint return does not imply trajectory constancy; a rotation supplies an explicit counterexample.

### Finite / exact witness layer

- \(\mathbb R^2\) rotation with constant radius-squared readout.
- two-level quantum phase evolution with constant Z probabilities and energy expectation but changing ray/X expectation.
- inertial relativistic worldline with constant four-velocity norm and changing event.

### Conditional standard-physics layer

- unitary time evolution is supplied by standard QM.
- timelike proper-time parameterization and four-velocity normalization are supplied by standard relativity.

### Unresolved / not established

- no universal DSD conserved quantity is derived;
- no standard-QM invariant is identified with a DSD static aggregate;
- no relativistic invariant is identified with DSD strict equivalence;
- no physical law is derived from the fiber theorem;
- no claim is made that every DSD transition admits a continuous-time generator;
- no quantum-gravity implication follows.
