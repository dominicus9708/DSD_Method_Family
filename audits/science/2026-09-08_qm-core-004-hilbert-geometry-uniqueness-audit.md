## 2. Source lock from the four DSD papers

The current DSD core is intentionally representation-agnostic.

### Formation

Formation supplies typed admissibility, realization, partial assignment, operational-channel identity, and finite composition. The post-Stage-VI term space/component-term map remain supplied.

### Property

The general Property Axiom System does not require axes, lines, inner products, matrices, tensor products, or Hilbert geometry. Such structures enter through auxiliary carriers, prerequisites, or optional representations.

### Static aggregation

The analytic realization is Banach-valued and non-unique. Output geometry is supplied by the selected realization/bridge rather than fixed by the abstract property core.

### Dynamics

Geometric structure, metric, localization, constitutive laws, and realized-axis data are optional supplied specializations. General dynamics does not impose a Hilbert-state geometry.

Hence no contradiction occurs if multiple state/effect geometries survive the generic DSD core. The present audit asks exactly how much stronger the cross-paper quantum specialization must become before uniqueness is possible.

## 3. Minimal operational specialization used in the pressure test

For a normalized state carrier \(\Omega\) and an effect family \(E\), impose only the following application-level requirements:

1. normalized states form a bounded convex carrier;
2. effects are affine maps \(e:\Omega\to[0,1]\);
3. each admitted finite measurement context sums to the unit effect;
4. the admitted effects separate distinct states relevant to the model;
5. reversible symmetries may act on the pure-state set.

These requirements are stronger than the current generic DSD core because convex mixing and affine effects are additional operational-QM/GPT specialization data.

They are nevertheless far weaker than assuming a Hilbert space.

## 4. Exact non-Hilbert countermodel: square bit

Let

\[
\Omega_{\square}
=
\operatorname{conv}\{(1,1),(-1,1),(-1,-1),(1,-1)\}.
\]

Define affine effects

\[
e_{X+}(x,y)=\frac{1+x}{2},
\qquad
e_{X-}(x,y)=\frac{1-x}{2},
\]

\[
e_{Y+}(x,y)=\frac{1+y}{2},
\qquad
e_{Y-}(x,y)=\frac{1-y}{2}.
\]

Then each selected effect takes values in \([0,1]\) on all four pure vertices.

The contexts

\[
\{e_{X+},e_{X-}\},
\qquad
\{e_{Y+},e_{Y-}\}
\]

are normalized pointwise.

The selected effects separate every pair of pure vertices.

The reversible \(90^\circ\) rotation

\[
R(x,y)=(-y,x)
\]

generates a group acting transitively on the four pure vertices.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{bounded convex carrier}
+
\text{normalized separating effects}
+
\text{pure-state transitivity}
}
\]

does not imply Hilbert geometry.

The square state space is not affinely isomorphic to the qubit Bloch ball/disk: an affine isomorphism preserves extreme-point structure, while the square has four extreme points and a quantum two-level pure-state boundary is continuous.

This is an exact finite countermodel to Hilbert uniqueness at the present gate.

## 5. Continuous reversibility still does not finish the job

The square bit fails continuous pure-state reversibility because its pure-state symmetry group is discrete.

However, requiring continuous reversible transitivity is still insufficient to select **complex** quantum theory.

A two-level real quantum system has a disk-shaped normalized state space. Its continuous \(SO(2)\)-type reversible symmetry is transitive on the pure-state circle, and its positive cone is an example of the Jordan/spin-factor family rather than the full complex qubit Bloch ball.

Thus the next discriminator must involve composite-system structure, not only one-system symmetry.

## 6. Local tomography is a substantive discriminator

Let \(K(d)\) denote the real vector-space dimension of unnormalized states.

For complex \(d\)-level quantum theory,

\[
K_{\mathbb C}(d)=d^2.
\]

For two complex qubits,

\[
K_{\mathbb C}(4)=16
=
K_{\mathbb C}(2)^2.
\]

This matches the dimension expected if joint states are determined by local-product measurement statistics.

For real quantum theory,

\[
K_{\mathbb R}(d)=\frac{d(d+1)}{2}.
\]

For two real two-level systems,

\[
K_{\mathbb R}(2)=3,
\]

but

\[
K_{\mathbb R}(4)=10
\neq
3\cdot3=9.
\]

Hence real quantum theory contains one extra global parameter beyond the local-product parameter count in this case.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{local tomography}
}
\]

is not cosmetic. It separates at least this real-quantum alternative from complex quantum theory.

It is also not currently implied by DSD Formation Clause VII: DSD finite composition is a finite sum of supplied component terms, not a tensor-composite system axiom, and the static layer explicitly allows information-losing reductions unless injectivity is separately proved.

## 7. Known mathematical narrowing route

The external reconstruction literature supplies a clear ladder.

### 7.1 Homogeneity + self-duality

The Koecher–Vinberg route characterizes finite-dimensional homogeneous self-dual cones by Euclidean Jordan-algebra structure.

This still permits more than complex quantum theory: real, complex, quaternionic matrix algebras, spin factors, the exceptional Jordan algebra, and direct sums.

### 7.2 Pure transitivity can help produce self-duality

Barnum, Ududec, and van de Wetering (2023) show that, under their hypotheses, homogeneity together with pure transitivity yields self-duality and essentially Jordan structure.

Thus a DSD route based on dynamics/symmetry can plausibly reduce the amount of geometry that must be stipulated directly.

### 7.3 Local tomography narrows further

Barnum and Wilce (2012), under additional natural assumptions on systems and composites and the presence of a qubit, show that Jordan-system structure plus local tomography singles out finite-dimensional orthodox complex quantum mechanics with superselection structure.

Masanes and Müller (2011) give another operational reconstruction from preparation/measurement/transformation requirements, including local tomography and reversible-state-space requirements.

These are external theorems. They are not DSD results. Their relevance is to identify exactly which structural requirements DSD would need to derive or justify if it is to reconstruct the Hilbert carrier rather than merely host it.

## 8. Candidate DSD Hilbertization package

The pressure test suggests the following cross-paper quantum specialization targets.

### H1 — convex mixture compatibility

A DSD quantum-state property carrier should be closed under operational mixtures, with affine readouts.

### H2 — complete state/effect separation

The admitted effect/readout family should separate states on the declared reconstruction domain.

### H3 — continuous reversible pure-state transitivity

Within a fixed system type, any two pure states should be connected by an admissible continuous reversible dynamical path.

### H4 — homogeneous interior reachability

Appropriate reversible/order-preserving filters should act transitively on interior positive states, or an equivalent operational condition should be established.

### H5 — state/effect self-duality

The positive state cone and positive effect cone should be identified by a positive inner product compatible with the QWEIGHT pairing, if this can be derived rather than stipulated.

### H6 — composite-system rule

DSD needs an explicit system-composition object stronger than Formation finite addition of channels.

### H7 — local descriptive tomography

For composite \(AB\), equality of all admitted local-product readouts should imply equality of the global state on the declared reconstruction domain:

\[
\boxed{
\forall e_A,e_B:\;
\omega_s(e_A\otimes e_B)
=
\omega_{s'}(e_A\otimes e_B)
\Longrightarrow
s=s'.
}
\]

Equivalently, the joint local-product readout map must be injective on the declared global state carrier.

This is a direct DSD reconstruction statement and makes the connection to existing aggregate-kernel audits explicit.
